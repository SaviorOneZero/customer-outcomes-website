01 / The gap

Companies know what they sold. Can they prove the customer achieved what they bought?

CRM, product, support, and delivery systems show what was contracted, implemented, used, discussed, and escalated.

Activity is not evidence of value. A customer can be fully deployed and highly active while the business case behind the purchase remains unmet.

When that gap stays hidden, renewal becomes the first honest test. Teams react late, executive conversations lack substance, and credible expansion opportunities are missed.

Renewal surprisesWeak executive conversationsUnclear accountabilityMissed expansion

02 / The Promise Arc

A practical operating model for customer value.

The Promise Arc follows the customer from the reason they bought through the evidence that the result was achieved. Each stage gives teams something concrete to align around.

  1. 01

    Promise

    Identify the business result that justified the investment—not merely the product or SKU.

  2. 02

    Outcome

    Turn the original promise into explicit, measurable customer outcomes.

  3. 03

    Value Path

    Define the milestones, changes, adoption, dependencies, and stakeholders required.

  4. 04

    Evidence

    Capture defensible proof of progress and realized value.

  5. 05

    Cadence

    Review evidence throughout the lifecycle, not only at renewal.

  6. 06

    Decide & Act

    Make risks, opportunities, and the next accountable action clear.

03 / A clearer view

Move beyond customer activity. Operate around customer outcomes.

THE USUAL VIEW
  • What did we sell?
  • Did we implement it?
  • Are they using it?
  • Is the customer happy?
  • What is their health score?
→
THE PROMISEARC VIEW
  • What outcome did the customer purchase?
  • What must happen to achieve it?
  • What evidence proves progress?
  • Where is value at risk?
  • What should we do next?
  • Can we defend renewal—or expansion?

Evidence over scores

You should be able to explain why a customer is healthy, why an outcome is at risk, and what evidence supports that conclusion.

It complements the systems teams already rely on. It does not ask CRM, support, delivery, or product data to answer a question they were never built to answer alone.
